Comparative Analysis with Backdoored Retrievers for Prompt Injection Attacks on Retrieval
Augmented Generation of Large Language Models

Our implementation differs from the Backdoored Retrievers for Prompt Injection Attacks on Retrieval
Augmented Generation of Large Language Models paper in both goal and system design. The paper is mainly attack-focused: it shows how poisoned documents or compromised retrievers can force malicious context into RAG pipelines and influence final answers. In contrast, our implementation is defense-focused and deployment-oriented.

Instead of only showing the vulnerability, our system adds practical protections directly into the legal RAG pipeline. These include query normalization for legal shorthand, prompt-injection chunk filtering, trust-aware reranking, demotion of untrusted text sources, strict context-only prompting, and audit logging. This means our pipeline does not rely only on embedding similarity, but combines multiple retrieval signals before selecting context.

The main improvement over the paper is that we convert its threat insights into an end-to-end secure legal RAG implementation. The novelty is not a new attack model, but a defense-in-depth architecture that reduces the chance that poisoned or instruction-bearing chunks affect the final answer. Our work is also domain-specific, since it is tailored for Indian legal documents and legal abbreviations such as IPC, CrPC, BNS, and POCSO.

In short, Backdoored Retrievers for Prompt Injection Attacks on Retrieval
Augmented Generation of Large Language Models explains how RAG systems can be attacked, while our implementation shows how a real-world legal RAG system can be hardened against those attack paths. The strongest contribution of our work is practical security engineering: integrating retrieval defenses, prompt safeguards, and traceable logging into one working pipeline.
